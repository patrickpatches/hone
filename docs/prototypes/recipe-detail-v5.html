Before you start

A heavy steel or cast-iron pan is not optional. Non-stick can't reach the heat — it warps and releases fumes. Cast iron, carbon steel, or stainless works; just give it longer to heat.

Get your mise en place ready before the pan goes on. Smashing happens fast — you have about 10 seconds once the beef hits the heat. Portion the balls, spatula close, cheese sliced and waiting.

80/20 mince is the move. Leaner mince makes a dry burger. The fat renders and builds the crispy, lacy edge — that's what makes a smash burger a smash burger.

01
Mise en place
5 min

Divide your mince into 125 g balls per patty — don't pack them tight, loose is better. Dice the onion very finely, slice the pickles, and get the cheese out so it's at room temperature. Set everything within arm's reach of the stove before you light the burner.

WhyOnce the beef hits the pan you have 10 seconds to smash it before it steams instead of sears. There's no time to go looking for anything.
02
Heat the pan
5 min

Put the pan over high heat. Wait until it's just starting to smoke — 4 to 5 minutes on gas, longer on electric. Add a teaspoon of neutral oil and swirl; it should shimmer straight away.

Stage photo
WhyThe crust — the Maillard reaction — needs surface temps above 150°C. A lukewarm pan steams the beef grey; a screaming-hot pan sears it brown.
03
Smash & sear
2 min

Drop a ball of mince in the pan. Press it down hard straight away with the spatula — lean your weight in — and hold for 10 seconds. Season generously with sea salt. Don't touch it for 90 seconds. Lift when the edges are deeply browned and lacy.

WhyThe smash maximises contact with the pan, and more contact means more crust. The lacy edge is the whole point — that's where the flavour lives.
04
Cheese & steam
45 sec

Flip the patty and lay two slices of cheese across the top straight away. Add a splash of water to the pan and cover with a lid or foil for 30–45 seconds. Lift the lid — the cheese should be melted and glossy.

WhyAmerican cheese melts flawlessly because it's engineered to. The steam pocket finishes the melt without overcooking the beef. If you swapped to cheddar, give it an extra 15 seconds.
05
Toast the bun
1 min

In the same pan (don't wipe it — that fond is flavour), drop to medium heat. Press the cut sides of the bun face-down for 45 to 60 seconds until golden. Watch it — brioche burns fast.

WhyA toasted bun is structural as much as it is flavour — it won't sog out under the sauce and juices the way a raw bun does.
06
Build & serve

Bottom bun → sauce → diced onion → lettuce → patty (cheese up) → pickles → sauce on the top bun → close. Serve straight away — a smash burger is a 90-second window.

WhyOrder matters: onion under the patty catches the residual heat and softens slightly; pickles on top stay crisp. Sauce on both sides is non-negotiable.
Design rationale — v5 "The Pass"

v5 keeps every v4 decision and adds three patterns that the best mobile recipe and product-detail pages all converge on — plus the two changes you asked for at the top.

What you asked for

1 · "Makes 2 burgers" removed from the top
You set the quantity with the stepper a few lines down, so showing a fixed yield up top was both redundant and slightly contradictory (it said "2" before you'd chosen). Gone. The glance row is now a clean trio — time · effort · origin — three facts that don't change when you scale.
Engineer cost: trivial
2 · Origin shown as a flag
The third glance slot is now the cuisine origin with a small circular flag. It's drawn as an SVG, not an emoji — Android's emoji flags render inconsistently (some system fonts drop regional-indicator flags to two grey letters), and a recipe app can't gamble the look of every card on that. An SVG flag set looks identical on every device.
Engineer cost: small SVG flag set, one per launch cuisine

The three best-practice upgrades

3 · Sticky bottom "Start Cooking" bar
A recipe page is long. In v3/v4 the primary action lived only near the top, so once you'd read down to the method you had to scroll back up to act. v5 adds a bottom bar that fades in the moment the inline button leaves the screen — the single most-validated pattern on commerce and recipe detail pages (NYT Cooking, Airbnb, every checkout). The inline button stays for above-the-fold; the bar takes over below. One primary action, always reachable.
Engineer cost: a sticky View + scroll listener — medium
4 · Collapsing top app bar
As the hero scrolls away, a compact bar with back + the recipe title fades in. This is Material 3's large-to-small top-app-bar behaviour and it's what makes a long Android page feel native: you always know what recipe you're in and the back affordance is always there, without the hero eating the top of the screen the whole way down.
Engineer cost: Animated header on scroll offset — medium (RN has this pattern built in)
Carried over from v4
Elevated why-notes (no longer the quietest text on the step), the allergen/dietary strip, equipment collapsed-but-naming-its-blockers, the leftover nudge under the stepper, the story-telling glance row with active-vs-total time, one rust CTA, one gold section language, carousel → full method.
All as v4

Honest note on flags — this is the limitation worth naming

A flag works cleanly for a country cuisine: American, Italian, Japanese, Thai, Mexican, French, Indian, Malaysian. It does not work for a region. Levantine spans Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Palestine — there is no single honest flag for it, and per the project rules there is deliberately no Israeli labelling. "Australian — modern and indigenous-inspired" is also not a flag story. So the rule is: country cuisine → flag; regional cuisine → a neutral region glyph (the globe mark from v4) + the name. I'd rather show an honest globe for Levantine than fly a flag that misrepresents four countries as one. The demos below show both paths.
