Frame B · Cook (same dark, single-step instrument)

What this answers

Every move below is in direct reply to a weakness named in the critique.

1 · One place, two layouts (Patrick's call)

v7 originally split surfaces — warm paper for browse, OLED black for cook. Patrick's revision: keep one unified dark palette across both so Hone reads as one place. The meaningful distinction now lives in the layout — long editorial scroll vs single-step instrument — not in the colour. That's actually a stronger signal: layout differences are felt more than palette differences, and the app avoids yet another palette swing.

2 · The pantry signal is the new top of the page

The single best "should I cook this tonight?" answer is "you have 7 of 9 — you're missing two." That's your own kill feature, and on v7 it sits directly under the title. The two missing items are named, and one tap drops them on the shopping list. This is the move that visibly beats Supercook and Yummly — they don't surface their own data this confidently.

3 · "Look for" becomes the second-loudest element on every cook step

In cook mode, the doneness cue is set in Fraunces italic at 18sp on a gold-rail callout, directly under the instruction. Deliberately bigger and more present than the body text — because at arm's length, "what should it look like right now" is the most valuable sentence on screen.

4 · The doneness photo is the top half of every cook step

Golden rule #4 finally gets the real estate it deserves. Photo on top, copy below, timer below that, and a 74dp tap-anywhere "Next" button across the bottom — built for a knuckle, not a fingertip. The ghost step number watermark gives you orientation without eating space.

5 · The no-photo state stops apologising

On a recipe without a photo, the hero becomes a typographic Fraunces card with a thin terracotta rule and a faint glyph watermark — it reads as intentional editorial, not a placeholder note that advertises a gap.

6 · Substitution honesty is surfaced, not hidden

The "honest swap" line under a swapped ingredient (golden rule #5) is now visible inline, in a small ochre italic callout — so the trade-off is part of the recipe, not buried behind a tap.

Colour — one unified dark palette

Patrick's call after the first draft: keep browse and cook on the same scheme so Hone reads as one app. Honest reasoning below.

Why this is the right call

The original v7 split the palette — warm paper for browse, OLED black for cook — to chase food-photography legibility. Patrick overrode it to keep visual continuity across the app, and on reflection that's the smarter move: one less palette change after four prior ones, no jarring context-switch when the user enters cook mode, and the meaningful distinction between "decide" and "cook" lives in the layout (long scroll → single-step instrument) where it's felt more strongly anyway. The photo legibility concern can be addressed inside the dark scheme by giving the hero photo full bleed and a tight vignette gradient, which v7 does.

Type — Fraunces + Inter, both clean on Android

A modern variable serif paired with the body workhorse you're already shipping. Both render crisply on Android via expo-google-fonts.

Smash Burger The crispy-edged diner classic. Fraunces · variable · opsz 9–144 · wght 400–900 (italic incl.)

Why replace Playfair Display

Playfair has very high stroke contrast — the hairlines disappear under Android anti-aliasing at small sizes and on lower-density screens. It also has no optical sizing, so the same outline does double duty for 12sp captions and 38sp display, which is a compromise either end. Fraunces is a contemporary editorial serif with the same warm gravitas, but it's variable with proper optical sizing (opsz axis 9 → 144): the glyphs literally adapt their stroke contrast to the size you set them at, so 12sp body and 38sp title both render crisply on a mid-tier Pixel. Italic carries the chef-voice "Look for" cues with personality.

Why keep Inter for body and UI

Inter is the right call for everything non-display. It's variable, has true tabular numerals (essential for timers and quantities), ships flawlessly on Android, and you already have it loaded — zero cost to keep.

Android porting (engineer notes)

npm i @expo-google-fonts/fraunces // in mobile/app/_layout.tsx — add the variable face + the italic import { Fraunces_400Regular, Fraunces_700Bold, Fraunces_500Medium_Italic } from '@expo-google-fonts/fraunces'; useFonts({ ...Inter, Fraunces_400Regular, Fraunces_700Bold, Fraunces_500Medium_Italic }); // in tokens.ts — update fonts: display: 'Fraunces_700Bold', displayItalic: 'Fraunces_500Medium_Italic', sans: 'Inter_400Regular', sansBold: 'Inter_600SemiBold',

Total weight added at runtime: ≈ 110 KB on first launch (variable face + italic), cached thereafter. Tabular numerals via fontFeatureSettings: ['tnum','lnum'] on any Text rendering a timer or a quantity. Min font-scale set to 100% but support up to 200% (accessibility, per CLAUDE.md).

Ergonomics — built for the propped-up phone

Every cook-mode decision below is in service of one fact: you're standing at arm's length with your hands busy, glancing at a screen with glare, and you can't afford to look twice.

The cook-step layout, top to bottom

Top half (≈ 420dp) · doneness photo — what the dish should look like at this stage. Edge to edge. A ghost step number watermark gives orientation; a 6-segment gold progress bar tells you how far in you are.

Middle · step title (Fraunces 30sp), one instruction (Inter 17sp, line-height 1.55), the gold "Look for" cue (Fraunces italic 18sp — louder than the body), big tabular timer (56sp, gold, tabular numerals).

Bottom third · a 74dp full-width terracotta "Next" pill with the upcoming step title quietly inside it, plus a tiny back link below and three icon tools (scaling, ingredients, exit). The whole bottom region is your tap zone — designed for a knuckle, not a fingertip.

Hands-busy moves

Wake lock stays on through the whole cook flow (CLAUDE.md mandate). Haptic confirm on every Next tap. Auto-advance on timer expiry as an opt-in toggle in cook-mode tools — for the steps where you're already at the pan and don't want to find the screen. Long-press anywhere = pause (haptic) — a forgiving gesture that works without aim.

Failure modes designed for

If you scale mid-cook, the stepper is on the tools row inside cook mode — no need to leave the flow. If you swapped an ingredient, the affected step shows the override automatically with a small olive cue ("adapted for your cheddar swap"). If a step has no photo, the top half becomes a typographic plate that names the dish stage — the photo gap doesn't break the layout.

Safe to build — what to do (and not do)

This is a visual + IA redesign, not a behaviour redesign. No collapsing animated headers, no native-driver scroll animations, no sticky bottom bars on the browse page — the very things that crashed v5 under Fabric. Cook mode's "Next" button is a static, full-width button at the bottom of the layout (not pinned via animated transforms). The browse page is a normal vertical scroll. Every visual you see above is achievable with StyleSheet + simple state — no Animated, no Reanimated scroll handlers required.

What changes vs build #126

Display font (Playfair → Fraunces), some token brightening (terracotta + sage shifted slightly brighter for OLED legibility — see the colour panel), layout architecture (pantry signal up top, journey overview, method preview list, prep merged into Get-ready), cook-mode treatment (single full-screen step with doneness photo + huge tap zone). All content preserved; some content (the pantry signal, journey overview, honest-swap inline note) is surfaced from data you already store.

What stays exactly the same

Recipe data model. Substitution engine. Scaling logic. Ingredient/recipe ID space. Routing. Cook-mode wake lock + haptics behaviour. The tabs + pantry + shop screens (out of scope).

If approved — phased rollout

Phase 1. Token brightening (terracotta + sage variants for OLED) + Fraunces font + browse-page layout (no cook-mode changes). Ship behind a feature flag so you can A/B against build #126 on your own phone before committing.

Phase 2. Cook-mode redesign — single-step layout with doneness photo and big tap zone. Wake lock, haptics, auto-advance toggle.

Phase 3. Pantry signal wired live + missing-to-list action + honest-swap inline notes pulled from the existing substitution data.

Each phase ships independently and is reversible. No EAS build without your trigger.
