Your kitchen and your list — one place

Two changes from v1, both Patrick's calls: quantities now read in the unit you actually buy (2 tomatoes, not 200g), and the three-number Need/Have/Buy dashboard is gone. An ingredient is only ever in one of two states, and the pantry just shows it.

No accounting. Every ingredient is in one of two states:
In your kitchen — you've got it. One tap sets it.
To buy — a recipe needs it, you don't have it. Shows the amount.
Need 14 · Have 9 · Buy 5  →  "Need" was never a number you read — it's just why an item is on the list. Your shopping list is simply the "to buy" items, filtered out of this same list.

Why two states, not three numbers

The system, simplified

One list, one tap

You asked: if we need an ingredient, it should show in the pantry. It does — right in its category, marked "to buy". When you've got it, one tap moves it to "in your kitchen". That tap is the only thing you maintain.

Buy what the shop sells

Real units, not recipe grams

Tomatoes are 2, not 200 g

Recipes are written in cooking units; shoppers think in shopping units. Each ingredient carries the unit you actually buy it in, so the pantry talks like a shopping list, not a recipe.

Recipe asks forPantry shows
200 g tomato2 tomatoes ≈ approx
5 cloves garlic1 bulb
handful coriander1 bunch

Honesty kept (golden rule 5): where a recipe weight becomes a whole count it's an estimate — marked "≈" because tomatoes vary in size. Things genuinely sold by weight (mince, flour, cheese) stay in grams. We never invent a precise number we can't stand behind. New work: a small "buy unit" reference per ingredient — count / weight / pack (bunch, bulb, tin), with an average weight for the count conversions. ~50–60 common ingredients to seed; the rest fall back to the recipe unit. This is the one genuinely new bit of data.
