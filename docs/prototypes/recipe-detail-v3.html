Before you start

Cast iron or heavy steel is not optional. Non-stick pans can't reach the temperature — they'll warp and release PFOA. Cast iron, carbon steel, or stainless steel frying pan works — just heat it longer.

Get your mise en place ready before you fire the pan. Smashing happens fast — you have about 10 seconds once the beef hits the heat. Pre-portion your balls, have the spatula close, cheese sliced and waiting.

80/20 mince is the move. Leaner mince = dry burger. The fat renders as it cooks and creates the crispy lacy edge. That's what makes a smash burger what it is.

Design decisions
Title moves inside the hero image
The current layout breaks the hero with a separate dark card below it — two things when one would do. Overlaying the title on a gradient inside the photo is how food magazines frame a dish. Photo + name = one editorial unit. The hierarchy is immediate: big photo, then the page.
Engineer cost: gradient overlay already exists — small
Attribution becomes inline text, not a pill
"Inspired by Andy Cooks · Watch original ↗" is credit, not an action. Making it a pill gives it the same visual weight as "Start Cooking". As inline gold text it reads as a byline — which is exactly what it is.
Engineer cost: remove pill, replace with Text — small
One primary CTA — Plan and Watch become ghost text
The current page has three pill-shaped buttons before you even reach the ingredients. Three equals = none dominant. "Start Cooking" is the boss. "Plan this recipe" and "Watch original" are secondary — they become ghost links, present but quiet. The user who wants them finds them; the user who doesn't isn't distracted.
Engineer cost: restyle two buttons — small
"Before you start" section — gold header, collapsible
The blue-ish header (sage token used as text) was failing WCAG AA contrast (3.7:1 — needs 4.5:1). Gold reads clearly. The section is important — "cast iron is not optional" matters — so it defaults to open. But repeat users who already own the right pan can collapse it. The chevron makes the behaviour obvious without an instruction.
Engineer cost: gold colour + Animated.View collapse — small
All section headers speak the same language
Before You Start, Ingredients, Method — all use the same gold uppercase Inter label + chevron. One pattern for every collapsible section. The user learns it once. Right now every section has its own visual dialect.
Engineer cost: one reusable SectionHeader component — medium once, zero after
Method shows a step preview strip before cook mode
Users commit more confidently when they can see the shape of the cook before they start. Six small cards scrolling horizontally show "here's the journey" in two seconds — the same reason recipe books show a process overview before the detailed steps. The step titles are already in the data; this surface is new.
Engineer cost: horizontal FlatList of step cards — medium
"Stage photos on the way" moved into Method section
Currently this notice lives near the top of the page — before the ingredients, before you've even decided to cook this recipe. It's an apology for something the user hasn't encountered yet. Moved into the Method section, it appears at exactly the moment it's relevant: when the user is looking at the steps.
Engineer cost: move component — trivial
Colour map: one job per colour, nothing else
Rust (#B84030): Start Cooking only. Gold (#F2CC2A): section headers, stepper accent, chef attribution. Emerald (#4FBF85): swapped ingredient only (from colour-refinement-v1). Sage (#3A7050): not on this page — sage = completion states, no completions here. Blue: zero. This is a browse page; it should feel calm and editorial.
Engineer cost: colour token pass on recipe/[id].tsx — medium
