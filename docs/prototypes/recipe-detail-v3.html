Smash Burger

The crispy-edged diner classic

20 min
Beginner
American
No leftovers

Before you start

Cast iron or heavy steel is not optional. Non-stick pans can't reach the temperature — they'll warp and release PFOA. Cast iron, carbon steel, or stainless works — just heat it longer.

Get your mise en place ready before you fire the pan. Smashing happens fast — you have about 10 seconds once the beef hits the heat. Pre-portion your balls, spatula close, cheese sliced and waiting.

80/20 mince is the move. Leaner mince = dry burger. The fat renders and creates the crispy lacy edge. That's what makes a smash burger what it is.

01
Mise en place
5 min

Divide your mince into 125 g balls per patty — don't pack them tight, loose is better. Dice the onion very finely. Slice the pickles. Get the cheese slices out so they're at room temperature. Set everything within arm's reach of the stove before you light the burner.

Why Once the beef hits the pan you have 10 seconds to smash it before it steams instead of sears. There's no time to look for anything.
02
Heat the pan
5 min

Place your cast iron pan over high heat. Wait until it's just starting to smoke — 4 to 5 minutes on a gas burner, longer on electric. Add a teaspoon of neutral oil and swirl. It should shimmer immediately.

🔥 Photo coming soon
Why The Maillard reaction — the crust — needs surface temps above 150°C. A lukewarm pan steams the beef; a screaming-hot pan sears it.
03
Smash & sear
2 min

Drop a ball of mince in the pan. Immediately press it down hard with your spatula — lean your weight into it — and hold for 10 seconds. Season generously with sea salt. Don't touch it for 90 seconds. Lift when the edges are deeply browned and lacy.

Why The smash maximises surface contact with the pan. More contact = more Maillard crust. The lacy edge is the whole point — that's where the flavour is.
