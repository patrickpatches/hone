What to know before you start

A heavy steel or cast-iron pan is not optional — non-stick can't reach the heat. Give it longer than you think to get screaming hot.

Have your mise en place ready before the pan goes on. Once the beef hits, you have about 10 seconds to smash.

80/20 mince is the move — the rendered fat builds the crispy, lacy edge.

Prep · have it ready

Portion the mince into loose 125 g balls — don't pack them.

Finely dice the onion, slice the pickles, get the cheese to room temp.

Method6 steps
1
Heat the pan
⏱ 5 min

Put the pan over high heat until it's just starting to smoke — 4–5 min on gas, longer on electric. Add a teaspoon of oil; it should shimmer at once.

Look forA faint wisp of smoke and oil that shimmers and moves like water.
WhyThe crust needs surface temps above 150°C. A lukewarm pan steams the beef grey; a screaming pan sears it brown.
2
Smash & sear
⏱ 2 min

Drop a ball in and press it down hard straight away — lean your weight in, hold 10 seconds. Season with sea salt. Don't touch it for 90 seconds.

Look forDeeply browned, lacy edges before you lift — that's the flavour.
Heads-upComing next: flip once, then cheese goes on immediately while it's hot.
3
Cheese & steam
⏱ 45 sec

Flip and lay two slices of cheese on top. Add a splash of water and cover for 30–45 seconds.

WhyThe steam pocket finishes the melt without overcooking the beef. Cheddar needs an extra 15 seconds.
4
Toast the bun
⏱ 1 min

Same pan, medium heat — press the cut sides face-down 45–60 seconds until golden. Watch it; brioche burns fast.

5
Build & serve

Bottom bun → sauce → onion → lettuce → patty → pickles → sauce → top. Serve straight away.

WhyOnion under the patty softens in the residual heat; pickles on top stay crisp.
Finishing & tasting

A smash burger is a 90-second window — plate the second it's built. Taste a pinch of the crust: it should be salty, beefy, and crisp. If the sauce is shy, add a little more on the top bun, not the bottom.

What changed — aesthetic only

This keeps every piece of content from the working build-#126 screen, in the same order. Nothing was removed and nothing new was added to the data. The change is purely how it looks and how the buttons feel — so it's a safe StyleSheet pass that cannot reproduce the Fabric scroll crash from v5.

Tactile, thumb-sized buttons
"Start cooking" is now a 54px rounded button with a soft rust glow and a real press state — it reads as the one thing to tap. The servings stepper, leftover chips, and swap pills all have ≥42px targets and clear pressed/selected states. Buttons that toggle (plan, favourite) show their on-state with a rust tint, not a colour you have to guess at.
One job per colour
The build-#126 screen mixed a raw #5B8FD4 blue (the "what to know" + stage notice) with the design-system sky token elsewhere — two different blues doing the same job. v6 unifies all information cues on the single sky token, and gives every other colour exactly one role (below). The result is calmer and reads as deliberate.
Calmer hierarchy & spacing
Hero gets rounded corners and breathing room; quick-facts sit in one clean divided row; sections use a Playfair title + a quiet count chip; the method steps get a clear round number badge and consistent callout rails. Same content, less visual noise.
Cues kept, just clarified
"Look for" (doneness) stays sage — a real go-signal. "Why" stays gold italic Playfair. "Heads-up" (what's coming) stays ochre. The amber Prep zone and the warm-brown Finishing band are kept and tidied. Nothing dropped.

The palette — each colour, one job

rust · Start cooking gold · stepper + "Why" sage · done / "Look for" ochre · Prep + "Heads-up" sky · information bronze · eyebrows warm brown · Finishing

For the engineer — why this is safe

This is a styling-only change to the existing browse-mode render in recipe/[id].tsx. No collapsing/animated header, no sticky bottom bar, no scroll listeners, no Animated on the native driver, no new schema fields. The v5 crash was a Fabric native-driver scroll animation on open — none of that exists here. Map these visuals onto the current StyleSheet; the component tree, state, and data stay exactly as build #126.
