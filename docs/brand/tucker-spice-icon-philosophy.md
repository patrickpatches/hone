# Warm Authority — the Tucker & Spice mark

**Philosophy.** A kitchen run by a calm master speaks softly and stands certain. The mark is that voice made visible: a single confident gesture on near-black ground, the way a good dish arrives plated with nothing extra. Authority through restraint, warmth through colour, recognition through one perfect glyph.

**Form & space.** The square is treated as a plate, not a poster — the mark sits dead-centre with generous dark around it, breathing. One hero element, the serif monogram **T&S**, carries everything. No frame, no clutter, no thin lines that vanish at thumbnail size. The strokes are full and editorial, cut with the optical contrast of a high-end type specimen, so the form reads as *crafted metal*, not drawn shape — the product of a master's patient hand.

**Colour & material.** The ground is warm charcoal deepened to an ember gradient — the look of a cookbook printed on near-black paper, lit from within. The letters are struck in warm gold, the colour of a finished crust; the ampersand alone burns magenta — the single spice, the pop that ties two words and signals the brand's quiet confidence. Cream is held in reserve. The palette is tiny on purpose: every colour does exactly one job.

**Scale & rhythm.** One dominant mark, optically centred (not mathematically — the serif's weight is balanced by eye). The glyphs nearly touch the safe-zone edge so the icon feels generous at any size, yet the core lives within the central sixty percent so it survives Android's circular crop intact. Tested mentally at 48px and at 1024px — it must read identically.

**Craft.** This is meant to look laboured-over: kerning settled by hand, the gold given the faintest warm glow so it lifts off the dark like embossed foil, the gradient calibrated so the corners recede and the centre advances. Nothing accidental. The result should feel like a maker's mark stamped into the base of fine cookware — meticulous, expert, inevitable.
